<h2 id="Summary-and-Conclusion">Summary and Conclusion¶</h2><p>Key findings</p>
  1. Distribution: Most stations hold 0 to 10 bikes, and the distribution is right-skewed. The mean (6.4) is above the median (5.0) because a few stations hold many more bikes, up to about 50.
  2. Where stations are: The South-West and North-East areas have the most stations 257 each. South-East and North-West tie for the fewest 176 each. The map shows stations are mostly in central DC (around longitude -77.0 to -77.1 and latitude 38.9) and sparser toward the edges.
  3. Group differences: The Washington, DC region has the highest average number of bikes per station (7.5) and Montgomery County, MD (North) has the lowest (2.2), a gap of about 5.4.
  4. Growth: This download does not include install dates, so there is no way to analyze how the system grew over time. The dataset contains 866 stations after cleaning.
  5. Relationships: Capacity and bikes available have r = 0.38, a weak to moderate positive relationship. Latitude has almost no relationship with bikes or capacity (all |r| below 0.1), while longitude is weakly related to capacity (r = 0.32), so stations farther east tend to be somewhat larger.
  6. Outliers / surprises: The boxplot shows a few stations with 28 or more empty docks. 87 stations (about 10% of the total) have 0 bikes. The strongest correlations (bikes vs. pct_bikes, r = 0.82) are partly built in because pct_bikes is calculated from bikes and capacity.
<p>Answers to my introduction questions</p>
  • Question 1: Bikes per station are right-skewed, with most stations holding 0 to 10 bikes and half of stations having between 5 and 14 empty docks.
  • Question 2: Stations are densest in central DC and sparser toward the outer regions in Virginia and Maryland.
  • Question 3: Regions differ. Washington, DC has the highest average bikes per station (7.5) and Montgomery County, MD (North) the lowest (2.2), a gap of about 5.4.
  • Question 4: There is a weak to moderate positive relationship between station size and bikes available (r = 0.38). Larger stations tend to have more bikes, but capacity explains only a part.
<p>Limitations: Bikes and empty docks are a snapshot, not an average over time. The dataset has no install dates, so growth could not be studied. Some stations may be locked or out of service, and regions with few stations can have unreliable averages. Correlation does not show cause.</p> <p>Next steps: Join this with ridership or Census data, use DC wards or neighborhoods.</p>
